  <context>
    CAM-04's requirement text refuses to pre-commit: "Exact approach resolved by prototyping during
    Phase 3, not fixed in advance … Decided by human playtest feel, not on paper." Both candidates
    are fully implemented behind the `O` toggle, alongside a no-mitigation baseline, specifically
    so this is a three-way comparison in one session rather than a forced choice between two.

    03-RESEARCH.md Open Question 2 notes that neither technique was originally designed for this
    camera shape — third-person occlusion-fade assumes a much closer follow cam, and GTA1/2's
    steepen-on-approach solved the reverse framing. That is precisely why the answer is empirical.

    The choice is not permanent for the project: Phase 4 brings real city geometry at a scale these
    fourteen placeholder boxes cannot represent, and the ADR should say what would justify
    revisiting.
  </context>
  <options>
    <option id="fade">
      <name>Fade occluding buildings to translucent</name>
      <pros>Keeps the camera's framing and the sense of speed completely untouched; the car is never lost; scales trivially to many buildings</pros>
      <cons>Translucent buildings can read as a rendering bug rather than an effect; multiple overlapping fades need depth-sorting care; at real city density a large fraction of the frame could be translucent at once</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="steepen">
      <name>Dynamically steepen the camera toward near-overhead in dense areas</name>
      <pros>Every building stays solid and the world stays believable; matches the documented GTA1/2 precedent CAM-04's own text cites; the pitch change itself reads as deliberate chopper behaviour</pros>
      <cons>Changing pitch changes framing, which is the exact lever SC4's speed legibility depends on — steepening could cost sense of speed in precisely the dense areas where a chase is most intense; needs the fan-ray density probe every frame</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="both">
      <name>Both — fade as the primary, with steepen layered on above a higher density threshold</name>
      <pros>Fade handles the common single-occluder case cheaply while steepen handles genuine urban canyons; both code paths already exist</pros>
      <cons>Two effects firing together is harder to tune and harder to read; the playtest compared them separately, so this option is only honest if the session actually tried them combined</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="neither">
      <name>Neither — buildings are left solid</name>
      <pros>Simplest; no per-frame raycasting at all</pros>
      <cons>Directly violates CAM-04's requirement that buildings "never permanently block the view of the car or road"; only defensible if the playtest found occlusion genuinely never happens at this camera angle, which would itself need explaining</cons>
    </option>
  </options>
