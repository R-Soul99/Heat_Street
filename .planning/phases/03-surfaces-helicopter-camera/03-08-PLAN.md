of a camera that might not be the shipped one.

  <what-built>
    Plan 03-07 wired the six-surface test scene and the helicopter camera rig into the
    composition root. The camera derives its heading from the car's velocity (not chassis yaw),
    damps toward it with a tunable lambda, and shifts altitude, follow distance and FOV with
    speed. Both presentation skins and the low chase-cam fallback are selectable. Every camera
    tunable is a live slider in the `?debug` + `G` panel and persists across reloads.

    Automated evidence already in hand: the altitude/FOV curve is monotonic, clamps at both
    ends, is `NaN`-proof, and separates 60 mph from 110 mph by 7.0 degrees of FOV and 6.0 m of
    altitude on the shipped defaults. What no test can tell us is whether that READS as faster.
  </what-built>
  <how-to-verify>
    Run `npm run dev` and open the printed URL with `?debug` appended.

    Key map: Backquote = profiler HUD, G = tuning panel, T = telemetry panel,
    C = swap helicopter/chase rig, V = swap police/sports skin.

    1. SPEED LEGIBILITY (SC4, the go/no-go gate). Drive the long tarmac straight from the
       spawn. Hold a steady ~60 mph on the speedometer, look away from the gauge, then
       accelerate to ~110 mph. Looking only at the world, not the gauge: can you tell the
       difference on sight? Repeat in the other order. Then try it once with the HUD hidden
       entirely. Record a plain yes or no, and what specifically carried the signal (or failed
       to) — FOV, altitude, ground detail streaming past, or nothing.
    2. TUNE IT, then re-judge. Before answering "no", open `G` and sweep the
       `Camera > Framing` sliders — `lowFovDeg`/`highFovDeg`, `lowAltitudeM`/`highAltitudeM`,
       `lowDistanceM`/`highDistanceM` — and `Camera > Damping > framingLambda`. D-12 is
       explicit that the fallback only wins after "real tuning effort". Record the values you
       land on. They persist across a reload.
    3. DRIFT STABILITY (SC3). Get to roughly 60 mph, then provoke a big slide with the
       handbrake plus steering until the car is visibly sideways — aim for something like a
       40-degree slip angle. Watch the CAMERA, not the car. Does it shake, jitter, snap, or
       swing wildly as the velocity heading rotates? Per D-11 the correct answer is a smooth
       lag, like a chopper pilot tracking a car — not a rigid instant lock, and not a wobble.
       If it is too loose or too tight, sweep `Camera > Damping > headingLambda` and record
       the value that reads best.
    4. LOW-SPEED BEHAVIOUR. Come to a complete stop, then creep forward and reverse slowly.
       The camera must not spin, hunt or snap when the velocity vector is near zero. Then do a
       full-lock doughnut and confirm it does not whip around.
    5. SURFACE TRANSITIONS (SURF-01, live). Drive the straight from the spawn all the way to
       the far end, crossing tarmac, gravel, dirt_road, grass, sand and mud in turn. Confirm
       each transition is FELT, not just seen — and specifically that gravel and dirt_road
       give the pronounced, dramatic slide CONTEXT.md D-05 names as the Dukes-of-Hazzard
       anchor, and that mud and sand stay controllable with normal inputs rather than bogging
       the car down (D-04). Record which surfaces feel wrong and in which direction.
    6. STRAIGHT-LINE STABILITY on loose surfaces. On the mud and sand bands, hold full
       throttle with zero steering for a sustained run. Confirm the car does not spontaneously
       spin out — this is `.planning/STATE.md`'s Phase 2 carry-forward, already covered
       headlessly by plan 03-06's stability sweep, now confirmed by hand.
    7. SKINS (CAM-03 / D-14 / D-15). Press `V` to cycle police/news and sports-broadcast.
       Confirm the two are clearly distinguishable, that the corner chrome label changes, and
       critically that NOTHING about camera distance, damping or targeting changes when you
       switch. Record which treatment reads better and what you would change.
    8. FALLBACK COMPARISON (D-12). Press `C` to swap to the low chase cam and repeat steps
       1 and 3. Record which camera makes speed more legible and which makes a drift more
       readable. This comparison is the evidence for Task 2's decision.
    9. FRAME COST (03-RESEARCH.md Pitfall 4). The in-game profiler HUD (Backquote) cannot see
       the skin's CSS filter cost — that is a browser-compositor operation outside three's
       render call. Open the browser's own performance panel and spot-check frame time with a
       skin active versus with the `#game` element's class removed via devtools. Record whether
       it is material.
    10. GAMEPAD (carried forward from Phase 2 SC2, unverified there for lack of hardware). If a
       gamepad is available, confirm proportional steering and analog triggers work and that
       the standard-mapping axis assumption holds. If no gamepad is available, say so and leave
       the item open — do not guess.
  </how-to-verify>
