Observations for the checkpoint:

- Every smoothness metric (suspension jitter, speed jitter, max tick drop) passes on every lane at both resolutions. Only contact loss and one vertical-jitter case fail.
- The flat baseline is almost perfectly quiet (vertical jitter 1e-5 to 2e-5 m/s), so 1.5 x baseline is far below the floors and the floors set every threshold. The bank 25 m/s failure (0.035 vs floor 0.02) is a 1.7x miss of an arbitrary floor, not of the baseline-derived value.
- subdiv 2 is clearly smoother on the hill lane (vertical jitter about 0.003 vs 0.008 at 15 m/s; no hill contact loss) but loses contact for longer on the climb lane at 25 m/s (10 vs 4 ticks).
- Climb contact loss at 25 m/s is the car launching off the hard 8% -> 0% grade break at z = +-100 that the course geometry specifies (a real road would have a vertical curve there). It is a course artifact, not collider seams.
- Flat regression: 5 of 6 routines match to under 0.004%. The `ramp` routine (scripted 120 mph launch, landing tilt measured 0.5 s after touchdown) differs 88% and, at 20.47 deg, would miss its own < 20 deg target on terrain. Investigated: the difference is specific to Rapier HEIGHTFIELD ground, not to the terrain code. Same flight until touchdown; after landing the rebound oscillates more (peak tilt about 20 deg vs about 11 deg). A flat 2-triangle trimesh and a thin cuboid both reproduce the legacy value (10.79 and 10.88); a 2x2 heightfield gives 12.5; the 400x400 heightfield gives 20.5; friction and FIX_INTERNAL_EDGES make no material difference (19.0 without the flag). It is not chaos: 1 mm ground shifts move the cuboid result by under 0.4 deg. Hard landings on a heightfield collider bounce differently from landings on a cuboid; this needs a decision (accept, tune landing assist for terrain, or use another collider type) at the checkpoint.
