## Course fix (what changed and why)

- Lane C's hard 8% -> 0% grade breaks (z = +-100) and its 0 -> 8% starts at z = +-400 are now **60 m parabolic vertical curves** (`GATE_VERTICAL_CURVE_M`, linear grade ramp over 60 m, so height is quadratic inside the curve). Plateau height is 24 - 0.04 * 60 = 21.6 m. At 25 m/s an 8% grade change over 60 m is v^2 * dg / L = 0.83 m/s^2 of vertical acceleration; the hard break was an impulse that launched the car.
- Length guidance for the map authoring road brush: size the curve as roughly 2.4 s of travel at the design speed per grade change (60 m at 25 m/s), and at least ~0.8 s of travel for gentle changes. Curve length needed scales with grade change x speed^2 (keep it well below ~1 m/s^2). Anything much shorter than ~10 m for an 8% change at 25 m/s will still launch.
- **Cause of the bank jitter:** course geometry, not the collider. The banked bend's exit straight rides lane C's centreline height, so the bank lane inherited the hard 0 -> 8% grade break at z = +400 (the bank lane's last 30 m). Smoothing lane C's profile removed it; the bank transitions themselves (linear bank-in/out over 30 m, hillside meeting the road edge) turned out not to be a significant source. Evidence: nothing else changed in the bank geometry and the jitter fell 3.5x at both subdivs.
- The centreline-height test no longer exempts points within 3 m of a grade break (no breaks remain; max error < 0.05 m on every lane point).

## Open decision for the checkpoint: `ramp` on a heightfield

Run 1 finding: the difference is specific to Rapier HEIGHTFIELD ground (a flat 2-triangle trimesh and a thin cuboid both reproduce legacy, 10.79 and 10.88). Same flight until touchdown; after landing the rebound oscillates more.

Bounded mitigation search (run 2, about 15 minutes, scratch tests since deleted; routine `ramp`, 400x400 flat heightfield with FIX_INTERNAL_EDGES, landing tilt in degrees; legacy = 10.88, target < 20):

| variant | ramp tilt |
|---|---|
| baseline | 20.47 |
| 800x800 grid (subdiv 2 size) | 20.88 |
| 200x200 / 40x40 grid | 20.62 / run did not complete (Infinity) |
| restitution 0 (Min combine) | 20.47 |
| friction 0.5 | 20.47 |
| FIX_INTERNAL_EDGES off | 19.05 |
| normalizedPredictionDistance 0.02 / 0.001 | 20.47 / 8.49 |
| collider contact skin 0.002 / 0.005 / 0.01 / 0.015 / 0.02 / 0.03 / 0.05 / 0.07 | 20.48 / 20.76 / 13.80 / 4.07 / 3.35 / 8.73 / 7.72 / 1.59 |

Findings:

- Restitution, friction and grid resolution do nothing meaningful. There is no cheap setting that reproduces the legacy 10.88 value.
- Contact skin and prediction distance DO change the landing, but non-monotonically (20.8 -> 13.8 -> 4.1 -> 3.3 -> 8.7 -> 7.7 -> 1.6), so the metric is a sample of a landing-rebound oscillation 0.5 s after touchdown that is hypersensitive to contact details. A skin of 0.02 or more would bring `ramp` under its own 20 deg target and leaves the other five routines unchanged (checked at skin 0.02 and 0.07: identical to the heightfield baseline to 4 digits), but it lands at 3.3 deg or 1.6 deg rather than the legacy 10.88, so it would still not meet the 1% flat-regression rule. It is a tuning accident, not a fix, and would change real-terrain contact for all driving.
- Options for the checkpoint: (a) accept that the hard-landing `ramp` routine is not 1%-comparable on a heightfield and gate it on its own absolute band (< 20 deg tilt, > 40 mph) rather than legacy equality, re-baselining it on terrain (the pass criteria of the original routine still need a value below 20 deg, which the heightfield baseline of 20.47 narrowly misses); (b) adopt a contact skin around 0.02 (needs a driving-feel check on the real terrain scene, not just this routine); (c) use a different collider type for terrain (trimesh with FIX_INTERNAL_EDGES matched legacy on flat ground but has the chunking and cost concerns noted in CLAUDE.md).
- Recommended: (a) plus a human landing check in the plan 06 drive, with (b) as the follow-up if hard landings feel wrong.
